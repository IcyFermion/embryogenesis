\caption{\textbf{Relaxing layerwise assignment constraints permits further
reductions in full-tree reconstruction costs.} Two complementary strategies
are compared for the measured 1,000-edge \emph{C.\@ elegans} protein lineage.
Layerwise assignment (blue) solves an exact linear assignment within each
bottom-up contraction round, preserving natural parent-slot multiplicities.
The degree-constrained spanning forest (green) greedily rebuilds connections
among measured internal cells and assigns terminals to the remaining openings.
Both use measured states and preserve four roots and 1,000 scored edges; the
forest enforces at most two children per parent. Curves retain non-dominated
cost pairs from each method's 301 sampled weights, without claiming a global
forest optimum. Costs are reported
in first-cousin-shuffle standard deviations and translated so the natural
lineage (black cross) is at the origin. The first-cousin null and fixed-topology
separate-clock Gaussian reference accompany a broad random-rebuild null on
continuous axes, without an inset or axis breaks. Random rebuild preserves
the four roots and provides an unoptimized reconstruction comparison; the
near-overlapping full-assignment shuffle and the internal-layer shuffle are
shown in the supplementary comparison. The Gaussian reference fixes four
measured root states and draws
independent, zero-mean increments along all 1,000 scored edges. Spatial
covariance scales with tracking duration; protein covariance is constant per
canonical parent--child transition. Full covariance is fitted within the
three-coordinate and 20-protein blocks, with zero covariance between blocks.
Separate clocks avoid dividing aggregate atlas protein differences by short
tracking intervals created by the $T\leq255$ cutoff: these protein states are
not endpoint observations aligned to the tracking embryo. All scored edges
span one canonical generation, and no additional clock parameter is fitted.
The panel displays 1,000 of 10,000 simulated lineages (seed 242); plus signs
mark reference-cloud means. The separate-clock mean protein cost is 3,604,
compared with 3,102 observed and 5,241 under the former shared-time Brownian
reference. The replacement is motivated by measurement-clock compatibility,
not by matching the observed total. Residual heavy tails and differences
between internal and terminal edges remain inadequately described; reference
displacement is not a biological efficiency effect size. Additional heuristics
and their constraint differences are documented in the supplementary figure
and inventory table. The terminal-only cache is withheld from plots pending
an edge-accounting and reconstruction-scope audit.}
\label{fig:ce_full_tree_collective}
